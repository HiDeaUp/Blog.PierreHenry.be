**Resilience and Mindset**

## How Rejection and Self-Labels Shape Our Lives

### Personal stories reveal how setbacks and limiting beliefs can close doors—or open new ones—depending on how we choose to see ourselves and our opportunities.

When rejection hits, it stings. Whether it’s being turned down for coffee or missing out on a job after several interviews, the first reaction is almost always the same: bruised ego, frustration, maybe even sadness. I’ve felt it myself. I remember asking someone out and getting a polite but firm “no.” At first, it hurt. My pride took a hit, and for a while, my heart felt heavy. But as days passed, the pain faded. A week later, I was already feeling better. A year on, I could look back and see things differently—what felt like a setback was actually making room for something better.

I experienced this firsthand with a job interview at Busuu in 2016. At the time, Busuu was still a relatively new company, and I was living in the UK. I went through four rounds of interviews. The final one was especially tough: I’d just returned from a trip to Vancouver, was jet-lagged, and had to be up at 4 a.m. for the call. Unsurprisingly, I didn’t perform at my best. A week later, HR let me know I hadn’t been selected.

It was hard to accept. I’d invested a lot of energy and hope into that opportunity. But with time, my perspective shifted. If I’d landed that job, I might have stayed in the UK for years. Instead, I moved to New Zealand, then Australia, where I met an incredible partner. Missing out on that job opened doors I never could have anticipated. Maybe that role would have led to other opportunities, but the path I ended up on brought me experiences and relationships I wouldn’t trade for anything.

The Labels We Give Ourselves

Rejection isn’t the only thing that shapes our lives—so do the labels we attach to ourselves. Maybe you’ve told yourself, “I’m not good at math,” or “I’m just not a logical thinker,” or “I’m bad with computers.” Sometimes these labels come from others, sometimes from our own insecurities. But once we accept them, they start to shape our choices and filter our opportunities. If you believe you’re not smart, you might avoid challenges that could prove otherwise. If you think of yourself as unlucky, you might not even notice the doors that are open right in front of you.

It’s a vicious cycle. The more we buy into these labels, the more we limit ourselves. Our unconscious mind absorbs these beliefs and acts accordingly, often without us realizing it.

Breaking the Cycle

The first step to breaking this cycle is awareness. Recognize the labels you’ve accepted—whether they were given to you or you gave them to yourself. These are not fixed truths, but mental barriers. Everyone has limiting beliefs, but they don’t have to define us. The key is to challenge them, to push beyond your comfort zone, and to keep expanding what you believe is possible.

Life is a series of opportunities, and to build a beautiful life, you need to fill your days with beautiful moments. That means being open to new experiences, even when they come disguised as setbacks. If you see yourself as unlucky or unworthy, you’ll close yourself off from possibilities. But if you remind yourself that you are more than your labels and rejections, you’ll start to see every day as a new chance to grow.

Your Mindset Is Everything

The hardest—and most important—work is on your mindset. Once you realize that the only real limits are the ones you set in your own mind, nothing can hold you back. Every day becomes an opportunity to break through mental barriers, challenge your limiting beliefs, and redefine what’s possible for you.

So, the next time you face rejection or catch yourself thinking in limiting terms, pause. Ask yourself: What if this is actually an opportunity? What if I’m capable of more than I think? The only way to find out is to keep moving forward, keep challenging yourself, and believe that you can go further than you ever imagined.
