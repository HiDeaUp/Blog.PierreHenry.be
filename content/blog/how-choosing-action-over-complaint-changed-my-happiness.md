**Personal Growth Choices**

## How Choosing Action Over Complaint Changed My Happiness

### Focusing on what you can control and taking small, consistent steps can transform your daily experience and lead to a more fulfilling life.

There’s a point in life when you realize you have a choice: you can see yourself as a victim of circumstance, or you can become the main actor in your own story. This decision—between resignation and agency—shapes not just your days, but your entire experience of happiness. I’ve experienced this firsthand, and I want to share how focusing on what I can control and choosing action over complaint has transformed my well-being.

## Victimhood or Agency: The Lens That Shapes Everything

It’s easy to slip into the role of the victim. When life feels unfair, the temptation is strong to say, “It’s not my fault—everything just happens to me.” I’ve been there. Sometimes, it really does feel like you were dealt a bad hand: born in the wrong place, at the wrong time, with little to fall back on.

But staying in that mindset keeps you stuck. If you see yourself as powerless, how can you improve? The real shift happens when you start to see yourself as the driver of your own life. There are always things you can’t change—random chance, the actions of others, the circumstances you were born into. But there are always things within your control, starting with your own perspective.

## The Power of Small, Consistent Actions

Here’s a concrete example from my daily routine. Every morning, I spend about 30 to 45 minutes developing a nutrition app called M Snap. The app lets you scan and track everything you eat—breakfast, lunch, dinner, even your Saturday night cocktail. It’s not a huge time investment, but the cumulative effect is powerful. By showing up every day, even for a short period, I see real progress.

This is the magic of the compound effect. Small, consistent actions, repeated daily, create momentum. It’s not about making dramatic changes overnight. It’s about choosing, every day, to do something that moves you forward.

## Focusing on What You Can Change

Living in Sydney, I’ve noticed something that always makes me smile: there’s almost no graffiti here. It’s a stark contrast to my time in Melbourne, where graffiti is everywhere. It would be easy to focus on the negatives, to complain about the things I don’t like. But I realized that I can’t single-handedly clean up every wall in Melbourne. If the graffiti bothers me that much, I can move—or I can choose to focus on the things I love about where I am.

This isn’t just about cities or street art. It’s about recognizing where your energy is best spent. Complaining about things you can’t change only breeds frustration. Instead, look for the areas where you can make a difference, even if it’s just in your own daily habits or immediate environment.

## The Glass Half Full

There’s always a choice: see the glass as half empty, or half full. I choose to look for the positive, to find a better way to live. Life is short—too short to waste on endless complaints. I want to live the most beautiful life possible, for myself.

Even the little things can be reframed. I walked past a hotel recently that had changed its name from Radisson to Paradox. Maybe the new name will bring a better reputation, a fresh start. The city is always improving, always under construction, and I choose to see that as a sign of growth rather than inconvenience.

## Acting Instead of Complaining

Today is November 1st. In Europe, it’s a public holiday—a day to remember the dead. Here in Australia, it’s just another Saturday. The city keeps moving, people keep working, and life goes on. If you work in a salon with me, your salary might double on weekends, but you still have to show up. Life doesn’t pause for our complaints.

I see so many people stuck in a cycle of negativity, always finding something to grumble about. But the more you complain, the worse you feel. The best way to feel better is to take action, to do something—anything—that moves you forward.

Not every action will help. Some things, like obsessively trying to fix what’s out of your hands, only add to your frustration. The key is to judge wisely: act where you can make a difference, and let go of what you can’t.

## The Gift of Balance

In the end, the greatest gift you can give yourself is balance. Focus on what you can control, and act with intention. That’s how you build a life worth living—a life that feels full, not empty.

If you’re ready to take that step, to act instead of complain, I invite you to join me in my workshops. Whether you’re looking for a more productive life or a deeper sense of well-being, there’s a path forward. But it starts with you, today, choosing to act.
