**Environment and Personal Growth**

## How Clean, Supportive Spaces Help You Achieve Your Goals

### Your surroundings can quietly limit or uplift your ambitions—choosing the right environment may be the key to lasting clarity and consistency.

It’s easy to overlook how much our surroundings shape our ability to build lasting habits and achieve what matters most. For a long time, I didn’t realize how the environment I lived in was quietly holding me back. The impact only became clear when I left and experienced something different.

Living in Melbourne, I saw firsthand how a city’s atmosphere can affect you. Like many big cities, Melbourne has its energy and opportunities, but it’s also marked by violence, vandalism, pollution, and a constant sense of grime. Walking down the street, you see overflowing trash bins, coffee cups scattered on the pavement, and graffiti that seems to multiply overnight. You might think you’re immune, that you can rise above your environment. But your unconscious mind absorbs it all, and over time, it weighs you down.

This isn’t unique to Melbourne. I’ve noticed the same pattern in other cities—Paris, Brussels, even smaller towns. The constant presence of dirt, vandalism, and insecurity creates a toxic backdrop that subtly restricts your ambitions. It’s like trying to grow a tree in poor soil: no matter how much you want to thrive, you’re limited by the ground you’re rooted in.

## The Power of a Fertile Ground

The difference is striking in places where nature dominates and the air feels lighter. Recently, I spent time in Cairns and Port Douglas. In Port Douglas, the serenity is tangible. Surrounded by nature, you don’t have to worry about crime or aggression. Sure, there are dangerous animals, but they’re only a threat if you provoke them—a far cry from the unpredictable dangers of city life.

In these environments, I feel uplifted and supported. There’s no one to harass you or judge you. The absence of aggression and filth isn’t just pleasant—it’s freeing. It allows you to focus on what matters, to be present, and to invest in yourself and your projects.

That’s been crucial for me. I’m currently working on a food-tracking app called Misnap, which helps you scan and log everything you eat. Having transparency about my diet is important, and developing this app requires clarity and drive. Could I have built it in a less supportive environment? Maybe. But I doubt I would have had the same energy and motivation I find here, where each morning I can choose a new café, feel safe, and start my day inspired.

Not every city is perfect. Even places like Gold Coast, which I loved for its mix of cafés, beaches, and forests all within walking distance, have their downsides—high costs, visible homelessness, and their own share of graffiti and vandalism. But the key difference is how these places make you feel. When you’re in a city that lifts you up, you’re more likely to pursue your goals with consistency and clarity.

## The Broken Windows Effect

There’s a lesson from New York’s “broken windows” theory: if you leave a single broken window or a bit of graffiti, it signals that neglect is tolerated, and soon more damage follows. But when a city is spotless, people are less likely to litter or vandalize. The environment sets the standard, and we unconsciously rise—or fall—to meet it.

I’ve seen this in places like Seoul and Bangkok, where, despite their size and density, the streets are remarkably clean. Sydney, too, stands out for its cleanliness and the sense of safety it offers. Of course, cities go through phases—what’s clean today might not be tomorrow, and vice versa. But the principle remains: you need to find your own fertile ground, the place that makes it easier to do what you love.

## Choosing Your Soil

You don’t always have to move to a new city to change your environment. Sometimes, it’s about finding pockets of positivity where you are—a favorite café, a park, a quiet corner where you feel safe and inspired. But if you have the chance to choose, pick a place that supports your growth.

Here, I can sit on a public bench with my laptop at any hour and feel completely secure. The cleanliness, the freedom to move, the birdsong in the background—all these details combine to create an environment where I can be my best self. It’s not just about comfort; it’s about giving yourself the best possible chance to build habits that last and accomplish what you truly want.

If you’re struggling to make progress, consider whether your environment is quietly holding you back. Sometimes, the most powerful change you can make is simply to plant yourself in better soil.
