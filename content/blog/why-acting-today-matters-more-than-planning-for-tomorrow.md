**Living with Uncertainty**

Most of us spend our days caught between the present and the future. I notice it in myself constantly: I’ll be at my desk, the sky outside gray and heavy, and my mind drifts—worrying about what’s next, what could go wrong, or what I should be preparing for. It’s easy to believe that life is happening somewhere else: in the next app, the next idea, or the next strategic move at work. We try to outsmart the future, convinced that if we plan enough, we can control what’s coming.

But the truth is, the future never unfolds as we imagine. No matter how clever our predictions, reality always surprises us. We think a colleague will make a certain decision, or our company will take a specific direction, but there are too many variables—too many unknowns—for us to really know. The only certainty is uncertainty.

This can be overwhelming. The anxiety of not knowing, of feeling unprepared, can weigh heavily on the mind. Yet the only moment we can actually influence—the only moment that truly exists—is right now.

## Clearing Mental Clutter

“Live in the present” is easy to say, hard to do. On gray days, when my mood matches the weather, I slip into a mental fog. Maybe someone spoke harshly to me, or I’m replaying a conversation that didn’t go well. It’s tempting to stew in these thoughts, letting them pile up until I can’t focus.

What helps me most is pouring all that mental clutter onto paper. I write down everything swirling in my head, no matter how trivial. I visualize flushing away the mental buildup—letting it go. This act of writing isn’t just venting; it’s a way to reclaim the present moment and make space for clarity and action.

The past is gone, and the future is unknowable. The only thing I can shape is what I do today.

## Building the Future, One Action at a Time

We often think we can construct our future by endlessly planning. But in reality, the future is built by what we do right now. Every action we take today—no matter how small—lays the foundation for what’s to come.

For me, this means investing in my own growth and knowledge. I ask myself: What can I do for myself today? Where can I invest my time and energy to build something meaningful? This isn’t about obsessing over what might happen, but about being present and intentional with what I have.

I keep notes—lots of them. I have over 9,000 notes in my Apple Notes app. It’s a love-hate relationship: the search function drives me crazy, but I keep coming back because it’s simple and always there. Sometimes, I can’t find what I’m looking for, no matter how many keywords I try. It reminds me that even our best tools have limits, and sometimes we just have to let go and move forward.

The lesson is simple: today is the only day that matters. The best way to shape your future is to act now, invest in yourself, and build your own foundation—one step at a time.

## The Paralysis of “What If?”

Worrying about the future can be paralyzing. I’ve felt it—the urge to freeze, to avoid making decisions because of all the “what ifs.” What if things go wrong? What if I make the wrong choice? This kind of thinking keeps us stuck, immobilized by fear.

But no one knows what will happen. The only way to move forward is to take action, even if it feels naïve or uncertain. Progress comes from doing, not from waiting for the perfect moment or plan. Inaction guarantees nothing changes; action, even imperfect action, opens the door to possibility.

The only real limits are the ones we set for ourselves. If you want to be the actor in your own life, you have to start something—anything. Don’t wait for perfection. Begin with what you have, where you are.

## Focus Over FOMO

It’s tempting to chase every new opportunity, to run after the latest shiny object. I see it all the time—people jumping from one project to the next, never settling long enough to make real progress. In engineering, we call this “context switching,” and it’s a surefire way to lose focus and dilute your efforts.

True progress comes from focus. Pick something and stick with it. Even if it feels imperfect, consistency builds momentum. I’ve learned this in my own projects, especially with my health and nutrition. For example, I recently developed an app called Milnaps—a nutrition tracker that lets you scan your foods and drinks. It’s my way of investing in myself and my future, one small step at a time.

Nutrition is another key to focus. Too much sugar wrecks concentration, and stress or fear can do the same. Taking care of your body is inseparable from taking care of your mind.

## Knowing When to Let Go

Determination is a virtue, but stubbornness can be a trap. I know this about myself: when I can’t find something, I can get obsessed, wasting time and energy chasing after it. There’s a difference between being persistent and being fixated on something that no longer serves you.

Sometimes, you have to let go. If a tool isn’t working, it might be time to switch. If a search yields nothing, move on. The important thing is to keep your focus on today’s actions, not yesterday’s frustrations.

## The Power of Small, Consistent Steps

Every day counts. Taking small, regular actions is the surest way to build the future you want. Invest in yourself, nurture your projects, and keep moving forward. Growth doesn’t happen overnight, but with steady effort, you’ll go farther than you ever imagined.

For me, writing things down—especially by hand—makes a difference. There’s something grounding about putting pen to paper, making your thoughts tangible. It’s a concrete act, a way to clear your mind and make space for new ideas.

So, focus on today. Take one step, however small, and trust that you’re building something worthwhile. I believe in you—100%.
