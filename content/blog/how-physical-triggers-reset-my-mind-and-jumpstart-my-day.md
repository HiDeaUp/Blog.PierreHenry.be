**Energy and Motivation**

Sometimes, the hardest part of the day isn’t the work itself—it’s finding the energy to start. I know the feeling: waking up sluggish, hitting an afternoon slump, or feeling foggy, like my mind is stuck in a jar. In those moments, it’s tempting to stay inside and hope the feeling passes. But I’ve learned that waiting rarely helps. Instead, I rely on a simple trigger to reset my energy and mindset: movement.

## The Power of Physical Triggers

Picture this: I’m in my apartment, not quite ready for the day. Maybe I have a headache, or maybe it’s just one of those low-energy mornings. The weather outside is cloudy and chilly, but I know staying put will only prolong the slump. So I make a conscious choice—I step outside for a few minutes of fresh air, head to the gym, go for a quick run, do some push-ups, or take a swim in the pool downstairs.

This isn’t just theory. I use it myself. Just this morning, after waking up around 2 a.m. (my schedule is a bit unconventional), I felt the urge to shake off the grogginess. The bay is only five minutes away, but with the cold and clouds, I chose a quick dip in the pool instead. Even though the water was chilly, the effect was immediate: I felt refreshed, clear-headed, and ready to tackle the rest of my day.

## Why Movement Before Mental Work Matters

You don’t need to be an athlete or have hours to spare. Even fifteen or twenty minutes of physical activity can make a real difference. Moving your body before mental tasks primes your brain for focus and creativity.

If you’re about to dive into a demanding project—designing a course module, writing, or tackling a freelance assignment—try this: before you sit down, do something physical. Go for a jog, climb a few flights of stairs, or just jump in place for a few minutes. If you have a gym or pool in your building, use it. But you don’t need fancy facilities. Sometimes I just put on energizing music and bounce around in my living room. A mini trampoline works, but so does dancing or doing jumping jacks.

The key is to create a ritual—a physical trigger—that signals to your brain it’s time to switch gears. Many freelancers and remote workers I know use this approach. They’ll hit the gym, grab a coffee, and then settle in for a productive work session. The coffee isn’t the magic; it’s the movement and the intentional transition.

## Making It Your Own

Here’s how you can build your own energy-trigger routine:

1. **Identify your low-energy moments.** Notice when you tend to feel sluggish—morning, afternoon, or after lunch.
2. **Choose a simple physical activity.** It doesn’t have to be intense. A brisk walk, a swim, a few push-ups, or jumping in place works.
3. **Pair it with something enjoyable.** Play your favorite playlist, open the window for fresh air, or use a speaker to boost the mood.
4. **Make it a habit before mental work.** Use this routine as a bridge between rest and focus, or between different types of tasks.
5. **Keep it accessible.** You don’t need a gym membership. If your building has a gym or pool, great—if not, use your living room, a nearby park, or your balcony.

For me, the ritual sometimes ends with a walk to a local café. Today, I’ll head to Genon Café in Elizabeth Bay, just a few minutes from my apartment in Sydney. It’s a small reward and another way to mark the transition from movement to focused work or reading. That walk, that change of scenery, is part of the process.

## The Environment Multiplier

Your environment matters. When your surroundings support your routines—whether it’s a gym in your building, a nearby park, or simply a space where you can move freely—it’s easier to stick to these energy-boosting habits. In cities like Sydney, Brisbane, or Dubai, it’s common to find buildings with shared pools or gyms, but even if you don’t have that, you can create your own supportive environment.

I explore this idea in depth in my workshop on environments that propel you forward. The right space can accelerate your success by making positive triggers effortless. If you’re curious, I invite you to check out more about designing an environment that energizes and supports your goals.

## The Takeaway

Don’t wait for motivation to strike. Use movement as your trigger. Whether it’s a swim, a run, a dance break, or a walk to your favorite café, these small rituals can transform your energy and set the tone for a productive, fulfilling day. I’m convinced—because I live it every day—that this simple shift can change everything.
