Each evening, as my day ends, I open my notebook and review what I have done. I retrace my steps, not to dwell on mistakes, but to see how I can improve tomorrow. This simple habit has changed how I work and live. My focus is not on perfection or on comparing myself to others. Instead, I iterate on my own process, refine my actions, and aim to build a life made of exceptional days.

## The Power of Iteration

Improvement is not a single event. It is a continuous process, made of small, deliberate changes that add up over time. Every day, I keep a list of processes and procedures for my regular tasks. After finishing a task, I reflect: What went well? What could have gone better? How much time did it take? I time myself, not to rush, but to understand my pace and spot inefficiencies.

This daily review is about more than efficiency. It is about adding value to my work. Sometimes, I realize a task could be delegated or done differently. Other times, I find a step I can automate or a new solution I can brainstorm. I schedule regular brainstorming sessions, often on weekends, where I review my notes from the week. For example, as a freelance developer, I might focus on how to create user interfaces for clients more efficiently. By improving my workflow, I can serve more clients, reduce errors, and free up time for new projects or investments.

## Compete With Yourself, Not Others

One lesson I have learned is to stop comparing myself to others. It is easy to measure progress against what we see on the outside, but this leads to frustration. Instead, I compare myself to who I was yesterday.

At the end of each day, I rate my performance from 0 to 20. If I give myself a 12, I do not judge. I investigate. I rewind the day, like watching a film in reverse, and ask: Why did I not score higher? Was I stressed? Did a task drain my energy? Did I skip moments of rest?

This honest self-assessment is not about blame. It is about understanding. I write down what could be improved, whether it is outsourcing a task that does not bring me happiness or making more time for relaxation. The next morning, I review these notes and set my intentions for the day. My goal is not a perfect score, but to move from a 12 to a 17 or 18, making each day better than the last.

## Designing Exceptional Days

A perfect life can seem out of reach, but it is simply a collection of good days. Each day is a chance to experiment, adjust, and grow. By focusing on what I can control-my actions, my mindset, my routines-I build a foundation for lasting happiness and freedom.

Here are the steps I follow:

- Keep a list of processes for recurring tasks.
- After each task or at the end of the day, reflect on what worked and what did not.
- Time yourself to find inefficiencies.
- Schedule regular brainstorming sessions to rethink your approach.
- Rate your day and review what could be improved.
- Set clear intentions each morning based on your reflections.

This approach is not about rigid discipline or relentless productivity. It is about shaping a life that fits you, one day at a time. By focusing your energy on what you can control and seeking small improvements, you create space for joy, creativity, and fulfillment.
