**Prioritizing Personal Goals**

There’s a specific frustration that sets in as the day unfolds. You wake up energized, convinced that today will be different. You stand in front of the mirror, reciting affirmations, feeling ready to take on your personal projects and make real progress.

But as the hours pass, fatigue quietly builds. After a long day at work—whether you commute by train, car, tram, or on foot—you arrive home drained. By the time you’ve eaten and caught your breath, your motivation has vanished. Instead of working on your goals, you end up scrolling online, watching others showcase their achievements and claim that success is easy if you just “do as they do.” The challenge you set for yourself that morning now feels out of reach. Days slip by like this, and procrastination becomes the norm.

It’s tempting to blame yourself, to feel inadequate for not meeting your own expectations. The affirmations fade, the pride rituals lose meaning, and you wonder if you’re simply not cut out for the life you want.

But the real problem isn’t your willpower—it’s the timing of your priorities.

## Why We Fail at Our Most Important Goals

Most people save what matters most for the end of the day. We promise ourselves we’ll work on personal projects after everything else, but by then, our mental energy is spent. The day’s stresses—unexpected demands, difficult conversations, minor annoyances—pile up, leaving little left for what truly matters.

To make real progress, you need to reverse this pattern. Your most important task should come first, before the world can drain your energy or distract you.

This means carving out time for your priorities right after you wake up, before your mind is cluttered with obligations. Even with family or other responsibilities, you can find creative ways to claim this time: get up earlier, head to a café or public library, or find a quiet spot before the day begins. Many cities have early-opening cafés or libraries—Starbucks at 6 a.m., for example—where you can work in peace.

If you live somewhere with good public libraries (I’ve found the best in the UK, Canada—especially Vancouver—the US, and Australia), these can be sanctuaries for focused work. Even in smaller towns, a quiet spot is often available. If not, co-working spaces or a cozy café can work just as well.

## The Power of the Morning

This isn’t just about logistics—it’s about psychology. Tackling your most important task first thing in the morning helps you avoid the mental clutter that builds up during the day. You sidestep unexpected problems and social distractions, giving yourself the best chance to succeed.

I think of it as “eating the frog.” If the hardest task is a big, ugly frog, eating it first thing makes the rest of the day lighter. If you wait, the frog only gets bigger as your energy fades, making it easier to put off.

By making your priority the first thing you do, you not only make progress—you free yourself to enjoy the rest of your day with a sense of accomplishment.

## Creating Space for Deep Work

Sometimes, an hour in the morning isn’t enough. For bigger projects or when you feel stuck, I recommend a “hermit weekend.” I regularly check into a hotel for a weekend, unplug from distractions, and immerse myself in deep work. This kind of retreat can be transformative, giving you the space and focus for real breakthroughs.

I’ve created a workshop on this method because I believe in its power. Whether you use my resources or try the idea on your own, the principle is the same: invest in yourself by creating intentional, undisturbed time for your most important work.

## Invest Your Time Where It Matters

The biggest mistake is letting your time slip away on things that don’t serve you—mindless scrolling, disorganized days, endless distractions. Prioritize your mornings for what matters most. Treat your time as the valuable resource it is.

And remember, learning only matters if you put it into action. Passive consumption—like watching a Netflix series—won’t change your life. Active learning, where you immediately apply what you’ve learned, leads to real transformation.

You don’t have to be perfect or start with every advantage. If you truly want something, you’ll find a way. Give your best energy to your highest priorities, and watch your life begin to change.
