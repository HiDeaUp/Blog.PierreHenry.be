**Pursuing Personal Ambitions**

## How I Stayed True to My Goals Through a Challenging Master’s

### Balancing financial, emotional, and physical costs taught me to prioritize my own ambitions and well-being over outside expectations.

It’s easy to lose sight of your own goals in a world full of outside influences. Social media, family, friends, and your environment can all push you toward ambitions that aren’t truly yours. You might set a bold intention—“This is who I want to become”—but when the work gets hard, it’s tempting to give up, convinced the path isn’t for you.

I’ve faced this myself. More than once, I nearly quit my master’s in data science. The workload was intense, the concepts difficult, and the rapid changes in technology—especially with AI—made me question if it was worth it. Sometimes, I’d stare at a topic, feeling lost—not just because I had to memorize complex material, but because I needed to understand the logic behind it. Why does this method work here but not there? What’s really happening under the surface?

To make sense of it, I started re-teaching what I learned. Explaining concepts, even just to myself, helped turn scattered, raw information into something clear and structured. This process forced my brain to connect new knowledge with what I’d already learned, making it stick for the long term. It’s effective, but in a field like data science, it takes time—and time is always limited.

### The Financial and Emotional Toll

There were moments when I seriously considered quitting. A master’s degree in Australia costs between $45,000 and $70,000—a huge investment. It’s easy to wonder if it’s worth it for a diploma. For me, though, the real challenge wasn’t the money. It was about keeping a promise to myself—a personal commitment, a test of resolve.

Still, the financial burden is real. Every dollar spent is a reminder of the stakes. But over time, I realized the most valuable resource wasn’t money—it was time. What would I regret more: the money spent, or the time lost not pursuing something meaningful?

### Balancing Ambition and Well-Being

There’s another cost: your health. Intense study can take a toll. You sleep less, eat poorly, and your well-being suffers. I learned that you only get one body, and neglecting it isn’t worth any degree.

To avoid burnout, I set strict boundaries. I limited study sessions to two hours at a time, using a timer on my Apple Watch. No matter how much work remained—unfinished assessments or looming capstone projects—I’d stop after two hours and move on. This routine kept me from falling into endless workdays at my computer.

From Monday to Friday, I studied two hours each day. On weekends, I’d extend it to six hours each day. That added up to about 32 hours a week—enough for steady progress without overwhelming myself. Some weeks, I did less, maybe four hours each weekend day, but I always aimed for the minimum needed to keep moving forward.

### The Power of Routine and Prioritization

It’s not just about the hours; it’s about how you use them. The start of any session is the hardest—it takes time to get into the flow and become productive. That’s why I focused on the most important tasks first: assessments and capstone projects, even if I didn’t always have all the materials.

Staying organized was crucial. I kept my priorities clear and updated my to-do list regularly with my favorite app. This helped me stay on track and ensured I was working toward what mattered to me—not just what others expected.

### What Do You Really Want?

Ultimately, the question isn’t whether you can keep up with your goals, but whether those goals are truly yours. Are you building the life you want, or following someone else’s script? The journey is never easy, and the temptation to quit will always be there. But if you set boundaries, prioritize your well-being, and stay true to your ambitions, you’ll find the strength to keep going—even when the path gets tough.
