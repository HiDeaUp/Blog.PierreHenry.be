**Rethinking daily balance**

Taking a midday pause in a nearly empty shopping mall, I realized just how much I needed a break. My legs were stiff from hours at my desk—a familiar feeling for anyone who spends their days in front of a screen. Last year, after breaking my kneecap, I spent three months unable to move my leg, relying on a brace and daily injections to prevent blood clots. That experience made it painfully clear how our bodies suffer when we neglect movement.

It’s easy to ignore the importance of simply getting up and walking, especially when deadlines loom. But real, sustainable balance starts with these small acts of self-care.

## The Myth of Perfect Planning

Many believe a balanced life is the result of meticulous planning. We try to schedule every moment, thinking better organization will bring peace. My experience says otherwise. Balance doesn’t come from rigid control; it comes from honoring your needs in the moment.

For example, I spend about 45 minutes each morning developing a nutrition app I created, M Snap. I started this project because I wanted more transparency in what I eat—something I value as much as freedom. With so much conflicting information online, I needed a tool to help me truly understand my food choices.

Even with a project I care about, I set strict boundaries. When my 45 minutes are up, I stop, even if I’m in the middle of something important. Making exceptions—just this once—quickly becomes a habit. One skipped break leads to another, and soon the chain of healthy routines is broken. I remind myself: it’s okay, I’ll finish tomorrow. This discipline keeps my life in balance.

## Lessons from Overwork

I didn’t always work this way. During my master’s in data science, I would lock myself in my kitchen all weekend, determined to study nonstop. My girlfriend sometimes went out, but I stayed behind, convinced that if I didn’t push myself, I’d fail. I’d paid a lot for that degree and felt I had to get top marks.

But this approach was miserable and counterproductive. Even with a solid background in programming, endless hours spent on R, Python, and Matlab wore me down. I didn’t allow myself to change my environment or take real breaks, and both my mood and my work suffered.

Looking back, I see how pointless this was. We only have one life, and torturing ourselves for success isn’t the answer. Everyone deserves a harmonious life. Balance isn’t a luxury; it’s essential.

## Redefining Deep Work

This philosophy is at the heart of my “Deep Immersion” method. Deep work isn’t about locking yourself away for endless hours. It’s about focused effort in short, intense bursts—never more than 90 minutes at a time. After that, it’s time to step outside, breathe, and reset.

This approach is kinder to your mind and body—and more productive. Trying to be productive all day leads to mistakes and burnout. We should apply this wisdom to our own lives, whether we’re learning, working, or creating.

## Making Work Enjoyable

One of the best ways I’ve found to maintain balance is to combine the useful with the enjoyable. When I worked for a company that allowed remote work on Thursdays and Fridays, I took the opportunity to travel. I’d book a flight to a Scandinavian country—Denmark, Norway, Sweden, or Finland—on Wednesday or Thursday night. I’d work from a BNB on Thursday and Friday, then spend the weekend exploring a new city.

This change of environment made even the less enjoyable tasks feel lighter. Instead of grinding through work in the same setting, I created experiences that nourished both my productivity and happiness. I recommend this to anyone: find ways to make your work environment uplifting, and don’t hesitate to seek out new surroundings.

## Practical Steps for a Balanced Day

If you want to bring more balance into your life, here’s what works for me:

- **Move regularly.** Don’t underestimate the importance of getting up and walking, even if it’s just around the block or through a quiet mall.
- **Set time limits.** Give yourself a defined window for focused work, and stick to it. When time’s up, stop—even if you’re not finished.
- **Protect your routines.** Avoid making exceptions to your healthy habits. One skipped break can quickly become a pattern.
- **Mix work with pleasure.** Change your environment, travel if you can, or simply work from a place that inspires you.
- **Listen to your body and mind.** If you’re feeling stuck or unhappy, it’s a sign you need to adjust your approach.

Balance isn’t about perfection. It’s about creating harmony between your ambitions and your well-being. You deserve a life that lifts you up, not one that drags you down.
