**Intentional Career Building**

It’s easy to fall into the trap of treating work as just a means to an end—a way to pay the bills, fill your days, and meet expectations. I know this firsthand. As a freelance data scientist and software engineer, I spent years taking on projects that didn’t excite me. The logic was simple: work is work, and sometimes you just do what’s necessary.

But over time, I realized that spending your life on uninspiring work is a hidden cost you can’t afford. Time isn’t just hours on a clock—it’s your life. Every moment spent on projects that don’t energize you is a moment you can’t get back. That’s why I now focus on building projects that genuinely excite me, that give me energy and purpose.

## Why Default Choices Hold You Back

Early in your career, it’s common to accept whatever projects come your way. Maybe you lack recognition or need to make ends meet, so you say yes to work that doesn’t interest you, thinking it’s just for now.

But if you keep following this path, you risk losing sight of what truly excites you. You end up living by default, shaped by others’ expectations. The real danger isn’t just boredom—it’s missing the chance to build something meaningful that reflects your passions and strengths.

## The Value of Filtering and Specialization

Instead, try filtering your opportunities. Be intentional about the projects and clients you choose. This isn’t easy, especially when you’re starting out and your reputation is still growing. It takes courage to say no to work that doesn’t fit, and narrowing your focus can feel risky.

Yet the benefits are significant. Specializing in a field or industry you care about—whether it’s health tech, blockchain, or another niche—sets you apart. You become an expert, and that expertise is valuable. It allows you to charge higher rates than someone who tries to do everything.

For example, if you’re passionate about a specific technology or sector, focus your work there. Over time, your knowledge deepens, your reputation grows, and your value increases. Clients come to you not just for your skills, but for your insight and enthusiasm. You’re no longer competing on price alone; you’re offering something unique.

## Continually Raise Your Value

This approach is about more than just picking the right projects. It’s about always asking: How can I increase my value? What can I do to deliver work that’s not just good, but exceptional? There’s always a way to raise your standards and offer something that exceeds expectations.

Maybe it’s mastering a new tool, refining your process, or finding creative solutions for your clients. The goal is to set your own bar higher than the industry standard and surprise people with the quality of your work. When you do this, you don’t just meet expectations—you redefine them.

## Balancing Immediate Needs with Long-Term Vision

Sometimes, you’ll need to compromise. Immediate financial needs might force you to accept projects that aren’t ideal. That’s okay—what matters is doing it with intention. Lowering your standards temporarily isn’t failure if you have a clear plan and vision for where you’re headed.

Know why you’re making certain choices. Maybe you need quick cash now, but you’re still moving toward a future where you only take on work that excites you. Don’t let short-term necessities become permanent habits. Keep your long-term vision in focus, and use every experience—even the less inspiring ones—as a step toward your ideal projects.

## Live By Design, Not By Default

The most important thing is to live by design, not by default. Don’t let society or circumstance dictate your path. Take time to reflect on what truly excites you, and build your work around that. Ask yourself: What projects make me feel alive? How can I deliver something that goes beyond what’s expected? Where do I want to be in the long run, and what steps will get me there?

It’s not always easy, and the path isn’t always straight. But when you commit to building what excites you, you unlock fulfillment and value that simply aren’t possible when you’re just following the crowd.
