**Intentional Living**

It was pouring outside—the kind of rain that soaks everything in minutes. Yet as I stepped out, I noticed something remarkable: birds still singing and flitting about, undeterred by the downpour. Tiny sparrows, feathers ruffled, seemed to relish the mysterious, almost mythical weather. I’ve always loved these moments, whether in the cool drizzle of Cairns, Australia, or near the glaciers of Saint-Joseph in New Zealand. Watching these birds embrace the day, rain or shine, reminded me how much intention matters in enjoying what we do.

Most of us don’t approach our days this way. Without a plan or routine, time slips away and we end up feeling lost. I’ve been there—sitting at my computer, unsure what to tackle next. That’s when it’s easy to fall into endless scrolling: Twitter, Facebook, YouTube. Hours vanish, and by evening, I’m left wondering, “What did I actually accomplish today?” Usually, not much—and that realization stings.

## The Trap of Passive Consumption

Online content is like fast food: you read a tweet, and a second later, it’s gone. You watch a video, and the details evaporate almost instantly. We spend so much time on these platforms, convincing ourselves we’re learning or relaxing, but really, we’re just filling the hours with forgettable noise.

I’m not immune to this. I’ve wasted countless hours on social media, chasing that fleeting hit of novelty. Even when something is genuinely interesting, it rarely sticks. The problem isn’t just the time lost—it’s the emptiness that follows. When you realize you’ve spent your day consuming and not creating, it’s easy to feel adrift.

## Building Structure: The Power of Routine

The antidote is simple: structure your days. Give yourself a guide, a routine, a set of actions to follow. When you know what you want to accomplish, you’re far less likely to waste time on distractions. For me, this starts every morning with a ritual: I open the M Snap app on my phone and track my food intake. It’s a small act, but I do it every single day, for thirty minutes to an hour.

M Snap isn’t just an app I use; it’s one I build and update myself. Being its first user keeps me accountable. Whenever I learn something new about nutrition or discover a feature that could help others, I update the app. Sometimes I hit a wall—unsure what to add next, facing the blank page—but the routine grounds me. Each day’s small effort accumulates, creating real progress over time.

The same principle applies to my other projects, like my Belle Vie workshop. I treat it as my second brain, updating modules whenever I discover something that works for me. These routines—morning and evening—are the backbone of my growth. Step by step, I move forward, and that steady progress brings genuine satisfaction.

## Create More Than You Consume

For every hour of content I consume, I aim to create at least half an hour of my own. Whether it’s writing, recording a video, or updating a project, the point is to balance passive intake with active output. If you only consume, you stagnate. When you create—even imperfectly—you build something lasting and meaningful.

This isn’t about perfection or grand gestures. It’s about consistency. Even if the content you create isn’t directly related to what you’ve consumed, the act of making something shifts your mindset. You stop being a passive observer and become an active participant in your own life.

## Build Your Future Today

The best way to predict your future is to build it—starting now. Invest in your projects, your routines, your life. Don’t wait for the perfect moment or for inspiration to strike. The birds don’t wait for the rain to stop before they sing; they embrace the day as it is. You can do the same.

If you want a life that reflects your values and brings you joy, start by organizing your days. Replace passive consumption with purposeful action. Create routines that move you forward, one small step at a time. The journey won’t always be easy, but it will be yours—and that’s what makes it worthwhile.
