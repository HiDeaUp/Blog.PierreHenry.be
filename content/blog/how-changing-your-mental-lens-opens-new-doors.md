**Mindset and Self-Growth**

Most people think success is about intelligence or relentless drive. But after years of working with people from all backgrounds, I’ve seen something different: the real barrier isn’t intelligence or motivation—it’s how we frame our thinking. This “haussment,” or mental elevation, is what determines whether we open doors for ourselves or close them before we even try.

How often do you tell yourself, “I don’t have enough time,” or “I can’t afford it right now”? These aren’t just passing thoughts—they’re self-imposed barriers. The truth is, you’ll never have “enough” time or money. Life always brings new obligations and expenses. The key isn’t waiting for the perfect moment, but making the most of what you have now.

## The Myth of Scarcity: Time and Money

Take money. If you set aside just 10 euros a day, that’s over 300 euros a month. It may not sound like much, but it adds up. Where do you find those 10 euros? Cancel your phone plan for a cheaper one. Pause your home internet and use free Wi-Fi at the supermarket. Skip the daily flat white—even though I love them, I’ve learned to go without when I need to save. Five euros here and there quickly becomes a significant sum. Even cutting back to one coffee a week can save you 20 euros a month. The point is to be vigilant: examine your spending and you’ll find room to maneuver.

The same goes for time. If you have time to shower and get dressed, you have time—at least five minutes—for your own projects. No one says, “I’ll go out naked because I don’t have time to get dressed.” You make time for what matters. So make time for yourself.

## Changing Your Mental Lens

The real shift happens in your mind. If you believe something is difficult, it will be. If you believe it’s doable, it becomes easier. Doubt is the cancer of the mind—the more you indulge it, the more it spreads. The antidote isn’t to wish doubt away, but to act. Action is the enemy of doubt. When you move forward, even in small ways, you prove to yourself that progress is possible.

Visualization helps, too. Each morning, take a few minutes to meditate and picture your ideal day. What will you accomplish this morning, this afternoon, this evening? What small changes can you make to shape your day into one you love—one that serves you, not the other way around? You are the pilot of your life, not a passenger. Without a clear direction, you’re like a bird flying aimlessly, or a taxi passenger who tells the driver, “Take me nowhere.” The driver will ask you to get out, because you haven’t given a destination.

## Setting a Course: Goals and Milestones

Just as you’d use a GPS to reach a distant destination, you need a roadmap for your life. Set a clear objective and a deadline—say, May 16, 2026. Then break it down: what can you do today to move closer? Each week, review your progress. What went well? What didn’t? Adjust and improve. It’s simple, but not instinctive, and certainly not something we’re taught in school.

This structure isn’t just about productivity; it’s about clearing mental clutter. Imagine your mind as a glass of muddy water. When something shakes you—a stressful event, a harsh word—the sediment swirls, clouding your judgment. The solution isn’t to react immediately, but to let things settle. Give yourself a day or two before making decisions. When the emotional mud settles, you’ll see clearly and act wisely.

## Emotional Clarity: Letting the Mud Settle

Everyone has a different timeline for this process. Some people need a week to recover from a shock; others bounce back in a day. The important thing is to know yourself and give yourself the space to let emotions subside. Acting in the heat of the moment rarely leads to good decisions. We say things we don’t mean, overreact, and often make problems worse. So pause, breathe, and wait for clarity. This is the real remedy for emotional management.

## Learning Together: The Power of Shared Growth

When I run my workshops, I don’t stand on a stage and lecture from above. I imagine you sitting right here with me, as if we’re sharing a table. This approach isn’t just more personal—it’s more effective. In Australia, where I’ve lived in several states, I noticed that restaurant servers often crouch down to take your order, meeting you at eye level. It’s a small gesture, but it makes a huge difference in how you feel—respected, heard, and equal. That’s the spirit I bring to my workshops: we’re in this together, learning side by side.

Helping others grow brings me immense joy and fulfillment. When I see my students making real changes in their lives, it motivates me to keep going. And I know that by working together, we both benefit.

The best time to act is always now. Don’t put off what matters to you. I believe in you—so believe in yourself, too.
