## How I Learned to Finish Projects by Acting Before Overthinking

### From building a simple app to maintaining a major open-source project, embracing action and flexible planning helped me overcome hesitation and make real

Every creator knows the feeling: you have an idea and the urge to build, but hesitation creeps in. Should you start now? Is it the right time? The more you dwell on these questions, the more doubt seeps into your days and stalls your progress. I’ve been there, and if you’re reading this, you probably have too.

Here’s what I’ve learned from years of launching projects, both big and small: if you want to finish what you start—and enjoy the process—you need to act before overthinking takes over. And you need to plan your days with intention, not just hope for progress.

## Start Before You’re Ready

If you have a project in mind, don’t wait for the perfect moment. The more you question whether it’s the right time, the more you risk falling into a cycle of doubt. Action is the best antidote to hesitation.

For example, I used to dread making YouTube thumbnails. It felt repetitive and uninspiring, but the default thumbnails were unattractive. Instead of letting that frustration linger, I built a simple app to generate thumbnails for me. It wasn’t a complex project, but it made a real difference in my workflow—what was once tedious became almost fun.

The project didn’t have to be perfect or ambitious. What mattered was moving from idea to action. Each small project you complete becomes part of your daily rhythm, building your skills and confidence over time.

## Define Your Day, Define Your Progress

It’s easy to drift through a day without a plan—especially on weekends or when you’re tired. You might sit at your computer, browse aimlessly, and end the day wondering where the time went. If you haven’t defined what you want to accomplish, nothing will change.

Real progress comes from deciding in advance what you want to achieve. If you keep doing the same things, you’ll keep getting the same results. Expecting a different outcome without changing your daily habits is wishful thinking.

Every day is an opportunity to invest in yourself. Even small projects—like my thumbnail generator—can give you something to look forward to. The more you build, the more you develop a unique expertise. Over time, this sets you apart.

## Plan Your Day—But Stay Flexible

A practical step that’s changed everything for me: I plan my day the night before. Not the week, not the month—just tomorrow. Planning too far ahead doesn’t work for me. My interests and priorities shift quickly, and if I try to map out a whole month, I lose motivation and forget what excited me in the first place.

By focusing on the next day, I keep my goals fresh and relevant. Sometimes, even within a single day, I’ll adjust my plans if something new captures my interest. The key is to give yourself direction without locking yourself into something that no longer fits.

Some routines—like exercise or healthy eating—benefit from long-term consistency. But for creative projects, short-term, flexible planning keeps me energized and engaged.

## Persevere for the Long Haul

Not all projects are quick wins. Some require years of effort and a different kind of commitment. For example, I spent over twelve years building and maintaining PH7 CMS, an open-source software project. It took thousands of updates—almost 11,000 commits. It wasn’t easy, and there were times when the work felt endless.

Sticking with a long-term project taught me the value of perseverance. Even now, I continue to update the software, though not as obsessively as before. Looking back, I’m proud of the persistence and energy I invested. Regular, steady effort can lead to something substantial and lasting.

## Know When to Pivot

It’s also important to recognize when a project no longer brings results or happiness. Sometimes, what once excited you simply isn’t for you anymore. Don’t force yourself to continue just because you set it as a goal.

Be open to letting go. If something isn’t working, ask yourself why you’re still doing it. Maybe it’s time to try something else and see each opportunity as an investment in yourself, not a rigid obligation. Iterating in this way keeps your work meaningful.

## Practical Steps to Move from Idea to Action

If you’re ready to break the cycle of hesitation and finish your projects, here’s what’s worked for me:

1. **Start now, even if it’s small.** Don’t wait for the perfect moment or overthink the details.
2. **Define your goals for tomorrow, tonight.** Give yourself a clear plan for the next day.
3. **Stay flexible.** Adjust your plans as your interests and priorities evolve.
4. **Commit to regular effort for long-term projects.** Perseverance pays off, even if progress is slow.
5. **Be willing to pivot.** If a project no longer serves you, let it go and invest your energy elsewhere.

Building a habit of action and intentional planning doesn’t just help you finish projects—it transforms the way you approach your work and your life. Over time, what once felt difficult becomes second nature, and the skills you develop set you apart.
