**Emotional Resilience**

## How I Stopped Letting Emails and Notifications Hijack My Day

### Breaking the cycle of digital distraction to reclaim focus, protect your priorities, and build a life aligned with your values—one intentional choice at a time.

A single email can hijack your entire day. You wake up feeling good, ready for your plans—a family outing, focused work, or a rare moment of calm. Then you check your inbox. Suddenly, a message triggers stress: maybe your server is down, your AWS account suspended after a spam attack you didn’t cause, or you receive a harsh critique or bad news. Instantly, your mood plummets. The day is no longer yours. Mental clutter takes over, clouding your thoughts and draining your energy—sometimes for days.

This isn’t just about the message itself. It’s about how we let unexpected, unwelcome events dictate our emotions and, by extension, our lives. Many of us are caught in a cycle of compulsively checking emails, notifications, and social feeds—hoping for something good, bracing for something bad. The more we check, the more we reinforce the habit, like a gambler convinced the next pull will be a win.

## The Addiction of Uncertainty

Constant checking shapes our days more than we realize. Each refresh or scroll feeds an addiction—not to the messages, but to the possibility of surprise. Most of the time, nothing happens. Occasionally, there’s a small reward: a kind note, a new opportunity. But it’s the unpredictability that hooks us. Like a slot machine, uncertainty keeps us coming back, even though the odds are against us.

This cycle fragments our attention, drains motivation, and leaves us vulnerable to emotional hijacking. The real question is: what is truly important to me? When we’re trapped in distraction and reaction, we lose sight of our priorities, letting the urgent crowd out the important.

## The Power of Notetaking and Simplicity

One tool that helps me break this cycle is a simple note-taking app. Over the years, I’ve tried many—Apple Notes for its simplicity, Inkdrop and Typora for developer features, Bear for its clean interface. The best app is the one that fits your workflow and doesn’t add complexity. I choose tools that are reliable and easy to maintain, rather than chasing trends.

Why does this matter? Organizing your thoughts and capturing insights clears mental clutter. It’s like flushing away stress and distraction, making space for clarity and focus. With a system for recording what matters, you’re less likely to be derailed by daily noise.

## Urgent vs. Important: The Eisenhower Matrix

It’s tempting to spend mornings on small, urgent tasks—cleaning, paying bills, replying to messages—thinking we’re being productive. But these tasks sap our energy, leaving us depleted for what really matters. The distinction between urgent and important is crucial. Urgent tasks demand immediate attention, but important tasks move us toward our goals and fulfillment.

I often use the Eisenhower Matrix to distinguish between urgent and important. Urgent tasks might include fixing a broken website or responding to a time-sensitive request. Important tasks align with your long-term vision: writing a book, building a business, investing in relationships or personal growth. If you always let the urgent take precedence, you’ll never get to what truly matters.

I’ve seen this in my own family. My mother loves her creative pursuits but often puts them aside to care for her grandchildren. There’s always a reason to postpone her own projects. Unless she draws a line and prioritizes her goals, those dreams remain on hold. It’s not selfishness—it’s recognizing that your time and energy are finite, and only you can decide how to invest them.

## Building Routines That Protect Your Focus

To guard against distractions and demands, I build routines into my day. I know what I’ll do in the first three hours, the next five, and so on. When working on something important—like writing a book—I use a separate computer account with minimal apps and no distractions. This “monotasking” helps me focus on one thing at a time, instead of scattering my attention.

I’ve also decluttered my phone, keeping only essential apps. The more apps and notifications you have, the more likely you are to be pulled away from what matters. Every distraction reinforces the habit of distraction, until it becomes part of your identity. You might start telling yourself—and others—that you’re just a “distracted person.” But it’s not a fixed trait. It’s a learned response to an environment designed to fragment your attention.

## The Company You Keep—Even Virtually

We are the average of the people we spend the most time with. Even if you can’t be around inspiring or focused people in person, you can immerse yourself in their ideas through books, podcasts, and videos. I listen to biographies and talks by people who inspire me, especially during commutes or walks. Even a half-hour a day can shift your mindset and help you feel less alone, especially if your path is unconventional or solitary.

## Discipline as Freedom

There’s a paradox here: discipline creates freedom. The more intentional you are about your routines, environment, and focus, the more space you create for creativity, growth, and joy. “Discipline equals freedom.” When you consistently invest in what matters—your projects, relationships, well-being—you build a foundation for the life you want.

I recommend checking email only once or twice a day, ideally after your most important work. For me, that’s around 1 p.m. and again at 6 p.m.—never first thing in the morning or late at night. This protects your mornings for deep work and your evenings for rest. Let your schedule shield you from chaos.

## Reframing: Turning Negatives Into Positives

No matter how disciplined you are, setbacks are inevitable. The key is how you interpret and respond. I’ve learned the power of reframing—looking at a negative event from a different perspective. Maybe you didn’t get the job you wanted, or a project fell through. In the moment, it feels like a disaster. But with time, you might see it as a redirection toward something better.

Our moods are often the result of our context. Happiness, sadness, anger—these aren’t random, but consequences of our environment and experiences. The ancient Greeks distinguished between “being happy” (a fleeting mood) and “happiness” (a life free from worry). While we can’t always control what happens, we can control the meaning we assign to events and the actions we take.

If you’re stuck in a negative loop, ask: What can I do now that’s proactive? What’s within my control? Sometimes, the best response is to take a walk, breathe, and let go of what you can’t change. Other times, shift your focus to the next meaningful task.

## The Lens of Perception

We don’t live in “reality” as it is, but in our perception of it. Our minds act like 3D glasses, coloring everything based on upbringing, culture, beliefs, and experiences. Two people can go through the same event and interpret it completely differently.

Our unconscious processes raw information and delivers a final perception, influenced by memories, emotions, and dreams. To live well, we must learn to change the “film” in our minds—to reinterpret events in ways that empower us.

This isn’t always easy. Sometimes, our brains fixate on the negative and amplify fears. But we aren’t powerless. By becoming the pilot of our own minds, we can choose the stories we tell ourselves, the meaning we extract, and how we respond to life’s ups and downs.

## Living in Harmony With Yourself

A beautiful life isn’t about avoiding problems or reaching a final destination. It’s about enjoying the journey, feeling at peace with yourself, and staying true to what matters. Every day brings new challenges and opportunities to practice these skills. The future is uncertain, but today is yours to shape.

If you want a life that reflects your values and aspirations, start by protecting your attention, clarifying your priorities, and reframing setbacks. Invest in yourself, one day at a time. The path may not always be smooth, but it will be yours.
