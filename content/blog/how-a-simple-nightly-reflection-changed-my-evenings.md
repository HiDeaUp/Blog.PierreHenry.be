**Evening Wellbeing**

## How a Simple Nightly Reflection Changed My Evenings

### Focusing on positive moments and gentle rituals can transform your nights from restless to restorative, one small habit at a time.

The evening should be a time of calm—a gentle transition into rest after the demands of the day. Yet, for many of us, it’s when our minds start racing. We replay awkward moments, dwell on unfinished tasks, and let small frustrations grow into anxiety that lingers long after the lights go out. But reclaiming your evenings doesn’t require a complicated overhaul. It starts with a single, intentional practice each night.

## The Power of a Positive Evening Retrospective

For a long time, I thought winding down meant scrolling through my phone or letting the TV play in the background. But I’ve learned that the real value of an evening routine lies in reflection—a deliberate pause to look back on the day.

Each night before bed, I take a few moments to review how my day unfolded. I ask myself: Did I enjoy the way today went? What moments brought me happiness, gratitude, or pride? Which parts of the day left me frustrated? The key is to focus on the positive. I make a conscious effort to highlight what made me feel good—those sparks of joy, gratitude, or fulfillment.

Why focus on the positive? Because dwelling on negativity at night fuels stress and anxiety. When you let your mind wander to what went wrong, you risk spiraling into rumination—not just about today, but about old disappointments that resurface in the quiet of the evening. This habit can sabotage your rest and set a negative tone for the next day.

Instead, ending the day by recalling what went well helps me drift into sleep with a sense of peace. It’s a simple shift, but it changes everything.

## Creating a Sleep-Friendly Environment

Reflection is only part of the equation. The physical environment you create in the evening also shapes how you feel as you wind down.

One of the most effective changes I made was adjusting the lighting in my home. Bright, blue-toned lights from screens and overhead fixtures can trick your brain into thinking it’s still daytime, making it harder to relax. That’s why I recommend switching to warm, yellow-toned lights in the evening. Most devices now have settings—like “night mode” or “warm light”—that reduce blue light exposure. You can activate these features on your computer or phone, or even set your screen to black and white for an extra calming effect.

If you want to take it further, consider investing in smart bulbs. I use LED bulbs from brands like Philips Hue and TP-Link. These bulbs can be programmed to dim automatically at a set hour, bathing your space in a gentle, soothing glow. They connect via Wi-Fi and are easy to control with an app. Since making this change, I’ve noticed a real difference in how relaxed I feel at night.

For those who spend a lot of time on screens, yellow-tinted glasses can also help, though I haven’t found a pair that matches my prescription yet. Still, for many, they’re a good option to reduce eye strain and signal to your brain that it’s time to wind down.

## Small Rituals, Big Impact

Beyond lighting, layering in small rituals can deepen your sense of calm. Playing relaxing music is one of my favorites. It sets a tranquil mood and helps me transition from the busyness of the day to the quiet of the night.

Breathing exercises are another powerful tool. I often practice a simple technique called the “4-4-4 triangle.” Here’s how it works:

1. Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds.
2. Hold your breath for 4 seconds.
3. Exhale, letting your abdomen deflate, for 4 seconds.

Repeat this cycle five or six times. This exercise, rooted in sophrology, helps slow your heart rate and quiet your mind. I find it especially helpful on nights when my thoughts are racing.
