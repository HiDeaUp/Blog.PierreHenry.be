**Personal Time Management**

We all know the feeling: staring at a packed calendar, convinced there’s no room for what truly matters. For years, I told myself, “I just don’t have time.” But after struggling with my own schedule, I realized that time management isn’t about finding the perfect system or squeezing more into the day. It’s about making honest choices and setting clear priorities.

## “No Time” Is a Choice

I’ve balanced a full-time job, a demanding master’s program, hours of programming courses, publishing books, and family life. My partner works long hours in a pharmacy, and our schedules rarely align. Still, whenever I say I don’t have time, I know deep down it’s a choice. If something truly matters, I can make space for it—even if that means taking a day off, blocking it out, and going into “hermit mode” to get it done.

This isn’t about superhuman discipline. It’s about being honest with myself. When I want to take a dance class, I sign up and pay in advance. That financial commitment makes me accountable—I feel obligated to show up and follow through. When you invest in something, you’re far more likely to stick with it.

## Making Time for What Matters

One of the most powerful changes I made was waking up an hour earlier every morning. I’d head to a café, order a (now shockingly expensive) flat white, and dedicate that hour to a personal project—like building my nutrition app, M Snap. That hour wasn’t “extra” time I magically found; it was time I intentionally carved out by going to bed earlier and protecting my mornings.

Money comes and goes, but time only moves forward. I can’t get back yesterday. That’s why I treat my mornings as sacred—an investment in myself and my goals. Whether it’s working on an app, writing, or simply reflecting, that hour is non-negotiable. It’s a daily reminder that my time is my life.

## The Cost of Sacrificing Sleep

For years, I thought I could cheat the clock. In my twenties, I’d stay out late with friends—sometimes until four or five in the morning—then show up for work on just a couple of hours’ sleep. My friends were students or worked part-time, but I had a full-time job in IT. I’d nap at my desk, push through the exhaustion, and tell myself I was being productive.

But over time, I learned that sleep isn’t optional. It’s the foundation for everything else. Now, I make a point of going to bed earlier so I can wake up refreshed and use my mornings well. Sacrificing sleep for more hours in the day is a false economy. Sustainable productivity—and genuine well-being—depend on rest.

## Invest in Your Time, Invest in Your Life

Managing your time isn’t about rigid schedules or squeezing in more tasks. It’s about aligning your days with your vision for your life. Every choice—what you say yes to, what you decline, how you spend your mornings and evenings—reflects your true priorities.

When you invest your time in what matters to you, you’re investing in your future self. That could mean committing to a class, setting aside time for a project, or simply protecting your sleep. The details will look different for everyone, but the principle is the same: your time is your most precious resource. Spend it on what helps you become the person you want to be.
