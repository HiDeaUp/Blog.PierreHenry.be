**Creative Project Momentum**

## How Building a Simple Tool Helped Me Finally Start Creating

### Turning a tedious task into a manageable habit broke my cycle of hesitation and taught me the value of daily, flexible planning for personal projects.

If you’re sitting on a project idea, waiting for the perfect moment to start, you’re not alone. I used to get stuck in endless questions—“Is this the right time? Should I do it? Or maybe I shouldn’t?” The more I hesitated, the more doubt shaped my days. Waiting for ideal conditions is a trap; action is the only way out.

One night, instead of overthinking, I built a simple app to generate YouTube thumbnails. It wasn’t glamorous, but it solved a problem that had been holding me back. Most of us don’t enjoy making thumbnails—it’s repetitive and easy to postpone. Yet, like a book cover, thumbnails matter. As creators, we often have to handle these tasks ourselves.

By creating a small tool that let me quickly select images, adjust colors, and generate thumbnails, I turned a tedious chore into something almost enjoyable. I experimented, generated dozens of options, and—most importantly—removed a mental barrier that had kept me from publishing more content. When a task feels overwhelming or boring, look for ways to simplify it. Sometimes, a small tool or a new approach can transform a dreaded step into something manageable.

It’s easy to drift through a day without a clear plan. If nothing is defined, nothing concrete moves forward. I’ve learned that if you don’t set a goal, you can’t expect anything to change. Every day is an opportunity to invest in yourself, but that only happens if you decide what you want to build.

For me, the key is to define my goals for the next day before I go to bed. When I wake up, I know exactly what I want to tackle. Without that clarity, even the most exciting projects can slip through the cracks.

I’ve tried planning weeks or months in advance, but my motivation fades quickly and my interests shift. That’s why I focus on planning one day at a time. Each evening, I decide what I want to accomplish the next day. This keeps my focus sharp and my motivation high. If my priorities change, I can adjust my plan—no guilt, no wasted effort.

Some routines—like exercise or eating well—are worth planning for the long term. But when it comes to creative projects, staying flexible and responsive to your own interests is crucial.

Not every project is meant to last forever. Sometimes you start something and realize it’s not for you, or it doesn’t make you happy. That’s okay. There’s no point in forcing yourself to stick with a goal just because you wrote it down. If a project isn’t working, give yourself permission to stop or to pivot.

I’ve had long-term projects that demanded years of effort. For over twelve years, I maintained an open-source CMS called PH7. It took nearly 11,000 commits and countless hours of coding. I’m proud of what I built and the persistence it took to keep it alive. But even with something that significant, I allowed myself to adjust my level of involvement over time. These days, I still update it, but not with the same intensity as before.

Persistence matters, but so does adaptability. If you discover that a project no longer fits your goals or brings you joy, it’s not failure to let it go. It’s growth.

The more you create, the more it becomes part of who you are. With repetition, building projects stops feeling like a struggle and starts to feel like second nature. You develop a kind of fluency that sets you apart. When you’re skilled at something most people avoid or find difficult, you become valuable. But this doesn’t happen overnight. It’s the result of regular action—making creation a habit rather than a rare event. Over time, you’ll find yourself more capable, confident, and motivated to tackle new challenges.

## Practical Steps to Move Forward

If you’re struggling to start or continue your own projects, here’s what’s worked for me:

1. **Stop waiting for the perfect moment.** Action breeds clarity and confidence.
2. **Simplify repetitive or complex tasks.** Build or find tools that make the process easier.
3. **Define your goals for tomorrow, tonight.** Don’t leave your day to chance.
4. **Plan short-term, stay flexible.** Adjust your plans as your interests evolve.
5. **Persist, but don’t be afraid to pivot.** Let go of projects that no longer serve you.
6. **Build habits through regular action.** Make creation part of your daily life.

The journey from idea to reality isn’t about grand gestures or perfect timing. It’s about small, consistent steps, a willingness to adapt, and the courage to start—even when you’re unsure.
