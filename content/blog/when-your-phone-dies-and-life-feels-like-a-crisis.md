**Perspective and Resilience**

It’s easy to overreact when your phone flashes that dreaded 2% battery warning. Suddenly, a minor inconvenience feels like a crisis. Your mind races: “How will I pay? What if I miss something important?” Negativity piles on, feeding itself in a vicious cycle. But what if, instead of spiraling, you paused and looked for alternatives? Maybe you have a backup card, or your smartwatch can handle payments. Perhaps, with your phone out of commission, you finally have a moment to read that book you’ve been carrying. In reality, the worst-case scenario isn’t so dramatic.

Putting things in perspective—relativizing—can break this cycle. When you’re stuck, whether it’s a dead phone or a bigger setback, reminding yourself that every problem has a beginning and an end helps you stay grounded. Even when things feel overwhelming, there’s always a way forward, and often, something good is waiting on the other side.

## The Trap of Focusing on the Negative

Consider the irony: you spend a fortune on a top-of-the-line phone, only to let a fast-draining battery sour your mood. Instead of appreciating its many features, you fixate on one flaw. This tunnel vision can happen anywhere in life. When you dwell on what’s going wrong, you miss out on opportunities and experiences that could bring you joy or growth.

Relativizing isn’t about pretending problems don’t exist. It’s about recognizing that setbacks are rarely as catastrophic as they seem in the moment. If your phone dies, you can do something else. If your hotel Wi-Fi is down, maybe it’s a chance to explore or enjoy a screen-free evening. Even in more serious situations—like facing the failure of a startup—there’s always another chapter. You can start over, try something new, and perhaps discover a path that suits you better.

## Turning Setbacks Into Opportunities

When you’re in the thick of a problem, it’s hard to see beyond it. But every challenge eventually passes. Losing a job, for example, can feel like a disaster. Yet, it might also be the push you need to pursue work that truly excites you or gives you more freedom. By reframing setbacks as opportunities, you open yourself up to positive change.

This mindset makes you more resilient and attracts better outcomes. When you stop feeding negativity and approach life with cautious optimism, you naturally draw in more good things. Over time, this subtle shift makes a real difference.

## Rediscovering Your True Passions

There’s another dimension to perspective: reconnecting with what genuinely excites you. Think back to when you were between eight and thirteen, before peer pressure and adult expectations crept in. What did you love doing? Maybe you spent hours drawing, building with clay, or playing with Playmobil figures. Those early passions often hold the key to what will fulfill you as an adult.

If you adored Playmobil as a child, what’s stopping you from bringing that joy into your adult life? Maybe you could collect rare sets, start a business selling vintage toys, or simply allow yourself to play again. These “true passions” aren’t just hobbies—they’re clues to the kind of work and activities that will make you happiest and most successful.

## The 80/20 Rule of Productivity and Joy

There’s a practical side to all this. When it comes to productivity, the classic 80/20 rule applies: focus on the 20% of tasks that yield 80% of your results. Often, these are the activities you genuinely enjoy—the ones that come naturally and energize you. The tasks you dread? Chances are, you’re not great at them anyway, and they drain your motivation.

The solution: delegate or minimize the tasks that sap your energy, and double down on your passions. When you do what you love, you’re not only happier—you’re also more effective. Your best work comes from enthusiasm and skill, not obligation.

## Perspective Is a Practice

Relativizing isn’t about ignoring problems or pretending everything is fine. It’s about seeing challenges in proportion, focusing on solutions, and remembering that every difficulty is temporary. When you approach life this way, you become more positive, resilient, and open to new opportunities. You also reconnect with your authentic passions, which are often the source of your greatest strengths.

So next time your phone dies, or life throws you a curveball, take a breath. Ask yourself: what’s the real worst-case scenario? What new possibilities might this setback reveal? And what did you love doing as a kid that could guide you now?
