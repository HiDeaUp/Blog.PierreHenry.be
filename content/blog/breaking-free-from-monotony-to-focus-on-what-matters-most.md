**Intentional Progress**

## Breaking Free from Monotony to Focus on What Matters Most

### How tracking your growth and making strategic decisions can help you reclaim your time and move forward with purpose.

It’s easy to lose sight of what truly matters when everything moves so quickly. The world has never advanced as fast as it does today. If you put off what’s important, you may find that “later” never comes. The best time to act is always now.

Recently, while compiling videos on my computer, I realized how monotonous the process had become. Each video felt like a repeat of the last. There were minor differences—one in English, another in French—but the core was the same. I was stuck in a loop, talking about the same topics and repeating the same points. This is a common trap: when you keep circling familiar ground, you stop evolving. Often, you don’t notice the stagnation until you finally ask, “What am I actually doing? Why does this feel like déjà vu?”

Tracking your progress is essential. Without a clear record of what you’ve done and how you’ve grown, you’re likely to keep spinning your wheels. I started looking at which videos performed better and asked myself why some had five times the views of others. Was it the topic, the preparation, or the delivery? This reflection isn’t just about chasing numbers—it’s about understanding what moves you forward and what keeps you stuck.

We all want to improve, but sometimes we hit a wall. That feeling of going in circles is a warning sign. By the time you notice it, you’ve probably been stuck for a while. When you sense that rut, it’s time to pause, reassess, and make a change.

For me, that meant identifying which videos I’d already made, which topics I’d exhausted, and even noticing patterns in my own body language. Of course, I can’t change who I am overnight, nor do I want to become someone else. But too much sameness leads to stagnation. Without new challenges, you stop pushing yourself, and growth stalls.

To break free, start by asking yourself: “What could I do differently?” Maybe it’s teaching a new subject or approaching your work from a fresh angle. I began tagging my videos—some already compiled, others still waiting for attention. This small act of organization helped me see where I was repeating myself and where I had room to explore.

This isn’t just a creative issue; it’s a strategic one. In business, when things aren’t going well, waiting only makes things worse. Inaction leads to debt—financial in business, but personal in life. Even if you’re not losing money, you’re losing something more precious: your time.

Time is the ultimate non-renewable resource. Lose an hour, and it’s gone forever. Money can be regained, but time cannot. Every moment spent on autopilot is a moment you’ll never get back. That’s why it’s crucial to use your time wisely—to act with intention and make decisions that move you toward what truly matters.

If you find yourself stuck in monotony, don’t wait for things to change on their own. Track your progress, challenge yourself with new goals, and remember: your time is your most valuable asset. Use it with care, and you’ll keep moving forward—toward the life you actually want.
