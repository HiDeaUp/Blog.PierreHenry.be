**Everyday Creativity**

## How I Train Myself to See New Possibilities in the Ordinary

### Creativity thrives when we combine familiar ideas and disrupt routines—here’s how breaking habits can help you spot opportunities and challenge invisible

Most people think creativity is a rare spark, reserved for artists or inventors. But in reality, creativity is simply the act of combining existing ideas in new ways. Whether or not your job demands it, weaving imagination into your daily routine can change how you solve problems and spot opportunities.

## The Art of Combining the Ordinary

Often, the most innovative products are just two familiar things merged unexpectedly. For example, I once looked at my coffee machine and wondered: what if it could also charge my phone wirelessly while I waited? Or imagine a toothbrush with a built-in mirror. These aren’t wild inventions—they’re ordinary objects, reimagined by pairing them differently.

This is the core of creativity: marrying ideas. Most innovations come from combining what already exists. Hanging flower pots from the ceiling, now common, was once a clever way to save space. What was once surprising becomes practical with time.

## The Invisible Limits We Inherit

If combining ideas is so straightforward, why do we get stuck? The answer is in the invisible boundaries we inherit. As children, we’re told not to color outside the lines or to keep our writing between the margins. These rules stick with us, shaping how we approach problems—even when they no longer apply.

We rarely notice these mental fences. We follow routines and accept “the way things are done,” often without questioning if there’s a better way. For instance, we assume a table must have legs that touch the floor, or that walking on our feet is the only way to move. Some conventions are practical, but many persist just because they’re common, not because they’re best.

## Breaking Routine to See Differently

To unlock creativity, disrupt your routines. One method I use is to deliberately invert my daily habits. Most of us wake up, use the bathroom, brush our teeth, shower, and eat breakfast in the same order. Try flipping that script: go outside before anything else, eat an unusual breakfast, or sleep on the floor for a night.

Sleeping on the floor, for example, has helped me rest better. It’s also how most people slept throughout history—beds as we know them are a recent luxury. Changing something as basic as where and how you sleep can shift your perspective and spark new ideas.

Breakfast is another example. In many countries, eating rice or fish in the morning is normal. What seems radical to you may be routine elsewhere. By borrowing habits from other cultures or eras, even briefly, you can break free from your own assumptions.

## Practicing Creativity Every Day

Make disruption a habit. Each day, try combining two unrelated things or approaching a familiar task in a new way. The more you practice, the easier it becomes to spot connections and generate ideas. Over time, questioning the status quo will feel natural.

Creativity isn’t about waiting for inspiration. It’s about seeking new combinations, challenging inherited limits, and daring to step outside your comfort zone. The possibilities are everywhere—if you’re willing to look.
