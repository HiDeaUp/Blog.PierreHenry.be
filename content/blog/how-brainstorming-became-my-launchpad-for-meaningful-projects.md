**Creative Process Insights**

You sit down, determined to do something meaningful—something that will make you proud and elevate your self-esteem. But there’s a catch: you don’t actually know what that “something” is yet. This uncertainty is the starting point of every ambitious project. How do you move from not knowing to clarity? For me, the answer is simple: brainstorming.

## Creating Space for New Ideas

When I’m searching for my next project, I don’t wait for inspiration to strike. Instead, I deliberately change my environment. I head to a café or a park—somewhere with energy or a touch of nature. A trendy café with music or a green space helps me think differently.

I order my favorite drink—maybe a flat white, a latte, or a smoothie. Sometimes, I put on noise-cancelling headphones and listen to binaural beats. These sounds seem to activate a quieter, background intelligence. In these relaxed but focused moments, “aha!” ideas often appear. Suddenly, I realize: “Yes, that’s the kind of idea I want to pursue.”

## Asking the Right Questions

Once I have the beginnings of an idea, I ask myself: Will this project make me proud? Is it something I’ll want to work on for months, not just days? Could it help me in my career or build my authority? I imagine myself executing the idea and pay attention to how it makes me feel.

To organize my thoughts, I bring a sturdy notebook and a pen or pencil. Writing by hand, especially in a new environment, grounds me. I spend half an hour, maybe an hour, jotting down ideas and questions. The goal isn’t a perfect plan, but space to explore what truly excites and motivates me.

## Finding a Project That Lasts

I’m searching for a project that will sustain my interest and energy for several months. This isn’t about chasing a fleeting whim. It’s about finding something that feels like an investment in myself and my life. When you land on the right idea, you feel it—a surge of motivation and the sense that you’re about to embark on something meaningful.

Brainstorming isn’t just a way to generate ideas—it’s the essential first step toward real progress. By intentionally creating the right environment, asking honest questions, and giving yourself permission to explore, you set the stage for projects that matter.
